FINDINGS OF THE COURT

[14] Having considered the evidence adduced by both parties and the submissions of counsel, the Court makes the following findings.

[15] On the issue of progress payments, the Court finds that the Defendant was in breach of contract. The contract clause 30.1 provides for payment within 30 days of the issuance of an Interim Certificate.

[16] The Defendant failed to pay within the stipulated time for Progress Claims No. 5 to No. 8, which collectively amounted to RM 4,800,000.00.

[17] The Court accepts the evidence of the Plaintiff's expert quantity surveyor, Encik Azhar bin Rahman, that the works were valued at the amounts certified.

[18] On the issue of delay, the Court finds that the Plaintiff was in concurrent delay caused by both the Defendant's non-payment and the Plaintiff's own scheduling issues.

[19] The Court applies the principle in Walter Lilly & Co Ltd v Giles Patrick Cyrill Mackay [2012] EWHC 1773 (TCC) that where there is concurrent delay, time is not at large and the Plaintiff is entitled to an extension of time.

[20] The Court grants an extension of time of 4 months to the Plaintiff, reducing the liquidated damages exposure.

[21] On the issue of defects, the Court accepts the evidence of the joint expert appointed by the Court, who found that only 23 of the 127 alleged defects were genuine and rectifiable.

[22] The cost of rectifying the 23 genuine defects is assessed at RM 180,000.00.

CONCLUSION AND ORDER

[23] For the reasons stated above, the Court makes the following orders:

(i) Judgment for the Plaintiff against the Defendant in the sum of RM 7,320,000.00 (being the outstanding progress payment of RM 7,500,000.00 less the rectification cost of RM 180,000.00);

(ii) Interest at the rate of 5% per annum on the judgment sum from the date of filing of the Writ of Summons until the date of full settlement;

(iii) The Defendant's counterclaim for liquidated damages is dismissed;

(iv) Costs of the action to the Plaintiff, to be taxed if not agreed.
