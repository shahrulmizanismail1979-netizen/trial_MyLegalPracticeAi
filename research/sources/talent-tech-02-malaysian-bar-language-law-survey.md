# LANGUAGE AND THE LAW

## INTRODUCTION

The official language of the courts is Malay which is the national language of the country. 
And that is universally accepted. English may be used with the permission of the Presiding 
Judge.

It is not uncommon for Pleadings to be submitted in both Malay and English. It is also not 
uncommon for written submissions to be made in English. Judgments are often written and 
delivered in English. Judgments reported in the Law Reports are often both in English and 
Malay. Malaysia incorporates part of the Common Law of England into Malaysian Law by 
Section 3 of the Civil Law Act of 1956. The whole of the law of Torts is found in the 
Common law. In addition English cases and cases from Common law countries interpreting 
statutes that are in *pare materia* (identical) to Malaysian statutes are of persuasive value. The 
Doctrine of Binding Precedent is applicable in Malaysia where like cases are decided the 
same way as previously decided cases have been decided by a Higher Court.

Given that an important source of Malaysian Law resides in the Common Law and there are 
tens of thousands of cases reported in various Common Law countries that would be useful to 
us as reference the English Language is an important language for Malaysian lawyers.

Former British colonies like India, Pakistan, Singapore, Hong Kong and many countries in 
Africa recognizing the usefulness of English continue its use alongside the local languages. 
English is regarded as a world language and no more the language of a former colonial power. 
There is discussion in India for English to be regarded under the constitution as an Indian 
language.

This survey is to determine the extent to which lawyers and Malaysian law schools fully 
appreciate the need for both English and Malay language proficiency.

The objective of this survey is not to question the place of Malay as the national language and 
the language of the courts but to ascertain/confirm the importance of English in the training of 
lawyers and in the practice of law and to try ascertain the extent to which policy makers and 
law schools recognize the need for lawyers to be proficient in both Malay and English.

## About the International Association of Forensic Linguists

The International Association of Forensic Linguists (IAFL) is an organization which primarily 
consists of linguists whose work involves them in the law. Narrowly defined, this means 
linguistic evidence in court (authorship attribution, disputed confessions etc.), but in fact the 
association aims to bring together those working on any aspects of language related to the 
law as well as members of the legal profession interested in linguistic matters like 
interpretation, police interviewing, courtroom interaction, etc.

Website: http://www.iafl.org/
